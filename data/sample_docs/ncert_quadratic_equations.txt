# NCERT Mathematics — Quadratic Equations (Class 10 Chapter 4 & Class 11 Chapter 5)

## 1. Definition of Quadratic Equation
An equation of the form:
a*x^2 + b*x + c = 0, where a, b, c are real numbers and a != 0,
is called a standard Quadratic Equation in variable x.

## 2. The Quadratic Formula (Shreedharacharya's Rule)
The roots (solutions) of the quadratic equation a*x^2 + b*x + c = 0 are given by:
x = [-b +- sqrt(b^2 - 4*a*c)] / (2*a)

## 3. Nature of Roots and Discriminant (D)
The expression D = b^2 - 4*a*c is called the **Discriminant** of the quadratic equation:
1. If D > 0: Two distinct real roots.
2. If D = 0: Two equal (coincident) real roots: x = -b / (2*a).
3. If D < 0: No real roots (Two complex conjugate roots: x = [-b +- i*sqrt(4*a*c - b^2)] / (2*a) in NCERT Class 11).

## 4. Relations Between Roots and Coefficients
Let alpha and beta be the roots of a*x^2 + b*x + c = 0:
- Sum of roots: alpha + beta = -b / a
- Product of roots: alpha * beta = c / a

The quadratic equation whose roots are alpha and beta is:
x^2 - (alpha + beta)*x + (alpha * beta) = 0
